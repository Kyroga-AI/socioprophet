# PART ONE: EXECUTIVE BUSINESS NARRATIVE

## 1.1 What SocioProphet Is

SocioProphet is a governed intelligence platform for regulated enterprises. It delivers a reusable set of AI capabilities — reasoning, retrieval, knowledge management, agent orchestration, provenance tracking, and evidence production — deployed across multiple product surfaces, customer types, and use cases, all running inside the customer's own environment.

The platform is not a single application. It is the intelligence infrastructure beneath multiple products and experiences. Organisations interact with these capabilities through purpose-built product surfaces — the most prominent being Noetica, a governed AI workspace — but the underlying platform serves a wide range of deployment patterns: direct enterprise use, channel partnerships, embedded intelligence layers, and managed services.

At its core, SocioProphet is a story about governing autonomous agents — not just governing chat. The architecture exists because AI is moving from "a person asks a question" to "an agent takes an action," and almost nothing in market governs that shift. Sovereignty and on-device deployment are how SocioProphet delivers this; they are not the whole story.

The defining characteristic: everything runs inside the customer's walls. No data leaves. Every AI action produces a cryptographically signed, replayable evidence record. The organisation owns its AI brain, can export it at any time, and can prove to any regulator exactly what the system did and why.

## 1.2 Why SocioProphet Exists — Three Inadequate Choices

Every regulated enterprise adopting AI today is choosing between three options — and none of them is complete.

**Choice one: use public AI.** Adopt Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini directly. Get frontier capability immediately. But sensitive context leaks into vendor infrastructure, institutional knowledge trains someone else's model, and every output is discoverable in litigation or regulatory review with no evidence trail to explain it.

**Choice two: build into one vendor.** Standardise on a single AI provider with deep integration and dedicated support. But model dependency locks the organisation in, workflow portability disappears, and when that vendor's roadmap diverges from the organisation's needs, there is no way out.

**Choice three: let teams build their own agents.** Give business units and technical teams the freedom to build AI agents wherever they see fit. Innovation happens at the edge — but execution fragments, governance is absent, costs become invisible, and institutional knowledge scatters into ungoverned workflows that nobody can audit.

**SocioProphet is the fourth path.** It isn't positioned against these three choices — it sits underneath all of them, as the governed, model-portable orchestration layer. Organisations can keep using public AI, stay with a preferred vendor, or let teams build agents — and SocioProphet governs all of it: capturing internal knowledge, preserving model choice, controlling agent execution, managing AI cost, and making every workflow auditable by design.

*Messaging discipline: never describe the other three paths as "wrong" — only "incomplete." This is what lets SocioProphet sit across every deployment mode instead of competing head-on with any one of them.*

## 1.3 The Business Problem

Regulated organisations cannot answer the question every regulator is now asking: **prove what your AI did, whether it was authorised, and how confident it was.**

Logs are not provenance. A log tells you a model was called. It does not tell you whether the output was retrieved from a document, inferred from a pattern, or generated without any grounding at all.

Dashboards are not replay. An aggregated risk signal does not let you reconstruct, step by step, why a particular AI-assisted decision was made.

The governance tools that exist today — Credo AI, Holistic AI, Modulos — govern on paper. They produce risk registers and model cards. They answer "what is our AI risk policy?" rather than "prove what your AI actually did."

SocioProphet answers the second question. Structurally. By architecture. Not as a layer on top — as the execution itself. This is the sharpest differentiator line in current use: **"Logs ≠ provenance. Dashboards ≠ replay."**

## 1.4 Why Now — The Market Shift

Three forces are converging simultaneously, creating a narrow window.

**Force one — adoption has outrun governance.** Nine in ten organisations use AI regularly, but only 39% report enterprise-level value from it (McKinsey, State of AI 2025). 75% have established an AI governance body, but only 12% describe it as mature (Cisco, 2026 Data Privacy Benchmark). The gap between "we're using AI" and "we can govern what it does" is the market SocioProphet was built for. [Source: McKinsey State of AI 2025, Cisco 2026 Data Privacy Benchmark — cited in seed deck, not independently re-verified by KYROGA]

**Force two — agents are multiplying the governance gap.** 75% of enterprise leaders are actively pursuing agentic AI, but only a small minority have moved beyond basic chatbot deployments (Forrester, June 2026). Citi built its own internal "Arc" system in April 2026 specifically to centralise agent control — evidence that even the most sophisticated institutions are building ad hoc governance because nothing purpose-built exists yet. [Source: Forrester June 2026 agentic AI report; Axios reporting on Citi Arc, April 2026]

**Force three — regulators are closing the window.** SR 26-2 (US, April 2026) places agentic AI explicitly outside existing model risk guidance — an instruction to govern it anyway, without a rulebook. APRA CPS 230 (Australia, amended April 2026) issued a direct industry warning that governance practices are not keeping pace with adoption. EU AI Act high-risk enforcement is live in 2026, with fines up to €35M or 7% of global turnover. FCA (UK) and MAS (Singapore) carry equivalent expectations.

These regulations do not prescribe a solution. They prescribe an obligation — govern your AI or own the consequences. SocioProphet is the solution to that obligation, arriving at the exact moment the obligation becomes unavoidable.

Separately, more than $100B has been committed globally to sovereign AI infrastructure in 2026, and 83% of companies now view sovereign AI as at least moderately important (Deloitte) — this is a board-level conversation, not an IT project. SocioProphet gives organisations their own AI brain — one that runs entirely inside their own environment, learns from their data alone, and produces a provable record of everything it does. No data leaves. The brain gets smarter over time. And the organisation can hand any output to a regulator and say: here is exactly what the AI did, why, and how confident it was.

# PART THREE: TARGET CUSTOMERS AND SEGMENTS

## 3.1 Primary Customer Types

SocioProphet serves organisations that need AI capability but face structural constraints on how they can use it — regulatory obligations, data sovereignty requirements, competitive IP protection, information barrier mandates, or professional liability exposure.

## 3.2 Segment Definitions

### Segment A — The Organisation Building Its Own Brain

**In plain language:** An organisation that wants to own its own AI capability — its own models, its own knowledge graph, its own evidence trail — running inside its own walls, maintained and upgraded on a managed cadence.

**Who they are:** Banks, asset managers, insurers, law firms, healthcare networks, government agencies, and large consultancies operating under prudential regulation, possessing proprietary institutional knowledge, with private cloud or on-premise infrastructure already in place.

**Buyer-specific pain — lead with the fear, not the architecture:**

- **Discoverability anxiety (buy-side asset management — e.g., Ya Ying / IFM):** Portfolio managers already build their own AI agents on models like Qwen and DeepSeek. The agents are brilliant and completely ungoverned. Their reasoning and IP are leaking into model providers, and a regulator like ASIC will eventually ask what's happening. The fear is legal exposure with no containment or replay.
- **Orchestration-at-scale governance (large banking / integration architecture):** Once AI moves from isolated pilots into high-volume automated workflows, the question becomes whether any individual decision inside that workflow can be traced and defended after the fact. The fear is scale outrunning traceability.
- **AI as operational, not optional (market infrastructure / capital markets — e.g., NASDAQ, Capital One):** For these organisations AI is already load-bearing infrastructure, not an experiment. The fear is invisible liability accumulating inside systems everyone already depends on — not whether to adopt AI, but what happens when nobody can explain what it did.

**Buying triggers:**
- Regulatory guidance or audit findings related to AI model risk (SR 26-2, APRA CPS 230, EU AI Act)
- A near-miss with AI-generated content (hallucinated citation, leaked client data, unapproved usage)
- AI governance moving to the board agenda
- A competitor deploying governed AI first and gaining referenceable advantage

**Business outcomes they care about:**
- Defensible AI governance they can demonstrate to a regulator
- Institutional knowledge captured, compounding, and protected as competitive IP — not filed away as a compliance cost
- Frontier-grade AI capability without data sovereignty risk
- An evidence trail that proves what happened at the decision level, not just the policy level

**Entry product:** Noetica (on-device or managed cloud)
**Revenue progression:** Freemium → seat/department subscription → enterprise fine-tuning engagement → managed model maintenance

### Segment B — The Software Provider Seeking AI Extension

**In plain language:** A mid-market software company that owns a deep data model in its vertical — its ERP schema, its compliance logic, its practice management workflows — and wants to add intelligent capability to its platform without building an AI team from scratch.

**Who they are:** Vertical SaaS companies, ERP vendors, practice management platforms, and compliance software providers with 50 to 500+ customers, a well-defined data ontology, and no credible AI roadmap.

**Pain points:** Customers asking for AI features they cannot deliver. AI-native competitors threatening their installed base. No internal ML team or budget to build one. Every bespoke AI integration is a $250K+ project with no template.

**Business outcomes they care about:** AI intelligence added to their platform in months rather than years. Customer retention and reduced churn risk. A new revenue stream from AI-enhanced capabilities. Defensibility against AI-native entrants.

**Why this segment matters strategically:** These organisations already own the ontology — the data schema and business logic — that makes domain knowledge ingestion work. One integration converts their entire customer base from a per-client discovery project into a template deployment. This is the primary channel strategy.

**Entry product:** Hellagraph integration plus Prophet Platform partnership agreement

### Segment C — The Enterprise Wanting Its Own AI Layer

**In plain language:** A large enterprise or software company currently paying Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google per-token for AI capability embedded in its products — and wanting to own the layer instead of renting it.

**Pain points:** Per-token costs scaling unpredictably. Dependency on a single frontier lab's pricing, availability, and roadmap. No portability. Inability to fine-tune on proprietary data without sending that data externally.

**Business outcomes they care about:** Cost predictability, independence from any single model provider's roadmap, sovereignty over the AI layer, competitive differentiation through proprietary fine-tuned intelligence.

**Entry product:** Prophet Platform (custom deployment). **Status:** Architecture ready; GTM is Stage 2 — not the current priority.
